How lunarcast works

This vignette explains the ideas behind lunarcast: why moving holidays are hard to model, what the classical calendar variation model assumes, where those assumptions break down, and how the proposed model repairs them. The mathematics is kept to what is needed; every formula is followed by its meaning in words.

library(lunarcast)

1. Holidays that move

The Gregorian year has about 365.24 days, the Islamic (Hijri) year about 354.37. Every Islamic holiday therefore comes about 10.9 days earlier each Gregorian year, and in monthly data it moves to the previous month roughly every 2.8 years. Lunisolar holidays such as the Chinese New Year and Easter also move, but within a band of about a month.

adha <- holiday_dates("idul_adha")
day  <- as.numeric(format(adha, "%j"))
year <- as.numeric(format(adha, "%Y")) + day / 366
plot(year, day, pch = 19, col = "darkorange", xlab = "", ylab = "Day of the year",
     main = "Eid al-Adha drifts through the Gregorian year")
abline(h = cumsum(c(0, 31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30)),
       col = "grey85")

Each grey line is the start of a month. A point that crosses a line means the holiday has moved to another month, which is exactly what a monthly model has to cope with. In 2006 the holiday occurred twice (10 January and 31 December).

2. The classical calendar variation model

The usual approach is a regression with ARIMA errors with a dummy for the month that contains the holiday:

Yt=β0+β1t⏟trend+St⏟seasonality+αVt⏟holiday+Nt⏟ARIMA error,Vt={1if month t contains the holiday,0otherwise.Y_t = \underbrace{\beta_0 + \beta_1 t}_{\text{trend}} + \underbrace{S_t}_{\text{seasonality}} + \underbrace{\alpha V_t}_{\text{holiday}} + \underbrace{N_t}_{\text{ARIMA error}}, \qquad V_t = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if month } t \text{ contains the holiday,}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}Yt​=trendβ0​+β1​t​​+seasonalitySt​​​+holidayαVt​​​+ARIMA errorNt​​​,Vt​={10​if month t contains the holiday,otherwise.​

The trend is either deterministic (the regressor β1t\beta_1 tβ1​t) or stochastic (differencing in NtN_tNt​). The seasonality is a set of monthly dummies, Fourier terms ∑k=1K{aksin⁡(2πkt/12)+bkcos⁡(2πkt/12)}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\{a_k \sin(2\pi k t/12) + b_k \cos(2\pi k t/12)\}∑k=1K​{ak​sin(2πkt/12)+bk​cos(2πkt/12)}, or a seasonal ARIMA error. Crossing the two choices gives six structures, m1 to m6, available in calvar_classic() and calvar_classic_all().

Two other calendar designs are common in applied work and are available through the argument design:

  • Lead–lag dummies (design = "leadlag"): separate dummies for the month before, the month of and the month after the holiday, α−1Vt+1+α0Vt+α+1Vt−1\alpha_{-1} V_{t+1} + \alpha_0 V_t + \alpha_{+1} V_{t-1}α−1​Vt+1​+α0​Vt​+α+1​Vt−1​.
  • Proportional windows (design = "window"; Bell and Hillmer, 1983; Lin and Liu, 2003): the share of the days of a window that falls in month ttt. For Eid al-Adha the default uses a window for the www days before the holiday, when the sacrificial animals are bought, and one for the holiday and the three tasyrik days:

The length www is chosen by AICc from 7, 14, 21 and 28 days. The three designs crossed with the six structures give 18 models: calvar_classic_all(y, dates, design = c("dummy", "leadlag", "window")).

The holiday part makes three assumptions:

  1. Timing: the holiday affects the month that contains it, fully, and no other month.
  2. Stability: the effect α\alphaα is the same in every year.
  3. Additivity: the holiday adds α\alphaα units, whatever the level of the series.

3. Where the classical assumptions break

The sapi series (Google Trends interest in “cattle”, which peaks before Eid al-Adha) shows all three problems.

Timing. Eid al-Adha fell on 31 July 2020, but the search peak was in August (79) rather than July (58): people search in the days before and after the holiday, and here most of those days were in August.

Stability. The size of the peak changed a lot. A simple descriptive measure is the highest month around the holiday divided by the median of the other months of the same year:

y     <- as.numeric(sapi)
yr    <- floor(time(sapi) + 1e-6)
hmon  <- holiday_window(adha, c(-3, 7), sapi)[, 1] > 0   # months touched by the holiday
ratio <- tapply(seq_along(y), yr, function(i) max(y[i][hmon[i]]) / median(y[i][!hmon[i]]))
plot(as.numeric(names(ratio)), ratio, type = "b", pch = 19, xlab = "",
     ylab = "Holiday month / median month", main = "The holiday effect changes over time")
abline(h = 1, lty = 3)

The effect is almost absent in 2004 to 2006, grows to more than three times the usual level in 2014 to 2019 and is smaller afterwards. A single α\alphaα averages these years and is wrong for most of them.

Leakage into seasonality (“ghost peaks”). Whatever the holiday dummy does not explain stays in the residuals of the holiday month. A seasonal autoregression such as Nt=ΦNt−12+εtN_t = \Phi N_{t-12} + \varepsilon_tNt​=ΦNt−12​+εt​ then predicts a part of that residual again twelve months later. When the holiday has moved to the previous month in the meantime, the model forecasts a peak in a month that no longer contains the holiday, and too small a peak in the month that does. Seasonal dummies suffer in the same way: they absorb part of the holiday effect into the “normal” level of the month.

4. Window regressors

lunarcast replaces the dummy by the share of a holiday window that falls in each month. For occurrence jjj of holiday hhh on date DhjD_{hj}Dhj​, the window is Whj=[Dhj+ah, Dhj+bh]W_{hj} = [D_{hj} + a_h,\ D_{hj} + b_h]Whj​=[Dhj​+ah​, Dhj​+bh​] and

whj,t=∣Whj∩Pt∣∣Whj∣,w_{hj,t} = \frac{|W_{hj} \cap P_t|}{|W_{hj}|},whj,t​=∣Whj​∣∣Whj​∩Pt​∣​,

where PtP_tPt​ is the set of days in month ttt. In words: count how many days of the window fall in month ttt and divide by the length of the window. The shares of an occurrence add up to one, and the window (ah,bh)=(0,0)(a_h, b_h) = (0, 0)(ah​,bh​)=(0,0) gives back the classical dummy.

w <- holiday_window(adha, window = c(-3, 7), y = sapi)
window(w, start = c(2020, 6), end = c(2020, 9))
#>            Jun       Jul       Aug       Sep
#> 2020 0.0000000 0.3636364 0.6363636 0.0000000

For 31 July 2020 the window runs from 28 July to 7 August: 4 of its 11 days are in July (share 0.36) and 7 in August (0.64). The window is chosen automatically from a grid of days before and after the holiday by AICc; for Eid al-Adha in sapi it is (−3,+7)(-3, +7)(−3,+7), for Eid al-Fitr (−28,+3)(-28, +3)(−28,+3), the last four weeks of Ramadan.

Several windows per holiday. One window gives one effect per occurrence. As in the proportional window design of the classical model, a holiday can also be described by several windows with their own effects, for example the days before Eid al-Adha and the holiday itself:

lunarcast(sapi, list(adha = adha),
          window = list(adha = list(before = c(-14, -1), holiday = c(0, 3))))

The holiday is then split into the parts adha_before and adha_holiday. Each part has its own window regressor, its own choice between a constant and a two-level effect and, if two-level, its own level-2 rule. The windows of a split holiday are given by the user; the automatic window search applies to holidays with a single window.

5. Multiplicative effects

lunarcast models zt=log⁡ytz_t = \log y_tzt​=logyt​ by default, so a holiday multiplies the level by eαe^{\alpha}eα instead of adding a fixed number. This matters when the level of the series changes: three times a low level and three times a high level are very different numbers of searches.

When only a share www of the window falls in a month, the natural assumption is that a share www of the extra activity falls there, so the level of the month is multiplied by 1+(eα−1) w1 + (e^{\alpha} - 1)\,w1+(eα−1)w. On the log scale this is achieved with the regressor

w~=log⁡{1+(eα−1) w}α,α w~=log⁡{1+(eα−1) w}.\tilde w = \frac{\log\{1 + (e^{\alpha} - 1)\,w\}}{\alpha}, \qquad \alpha\,\tilde w = \log\{1 + (e^{\alpha} - 1)\,w\}.w~=αlog{1+(eα−1)w}​,αw~=log{1+(eα−1)w}.

For example, if the full effect triples the level (eα=3e^{\alpha} = 3eα=3) and 64% of the window is in August, August rises by 1+2×0.64=2.281 + 2 \times 0.64 = 2.281+2×0.64=2.28 times, not by 30.64=2.023^{0.64} = 2.0230.64=2.02 times, which is what plugging www itself into a log model would give. Because w~\tilde ww~ depends on α\alphaα, lunarcast estimates the model, updates w~\tilde ww~ and re-estimates (two iterations are enough). With lambda = NULL the model stays on the original scale and the shares are used directly.

6. Two-level holiday effects

To let the effect change over time, every occurrence gets its own effect.

Level 1 (estimation):

zt=∑h∑jαhj w~hj,t+xt⊤β+Nt.z_t = \sum_h \sum_j \alpha_{hj}\,\tilde w_{hj,t} + \mathbf{x}_t^\top\boldsymbol\beta + N_t .zt​=h∑​j∑​αhj​w~hj,t​+xt⊤​β+Nt​.

The occurrence effects αhj\alpha_{hj}αhj​ are estimated jointly with the trend, the seasonality and the ARIMA error by maximum likelihood. A constant effect is the special case αhj=αh\alpha_{hj} = \alpha_hαhj​=αh​; lunarcast chooses between the two by AICc and requires at least six occurrences for a two-level effect.

Level 2 (forecasting the next effect). The effect of a future occurrence is not observed, so it is forecast from the sequence of past effects:

α^h,J+1=g(α^h1,…,α^hJ).\hat\alpha_{h,J+1} = g(\hat\alpha_{h1}, \ldots, \hat\alpha_{hJ}).α^h,J+1​=g(α^h1​,…,α^hJ​).

The same error measures how uncertain the next effect is, and it is added to the forecast variance (Section 9). plot() on a fitted model shows the estimated effects and the forecast for the next occurrence.

7. Trend and seasonal structure

The rest of the model, xt⊤β+Nt\mathbf{x}_t^\top\boldsymbol\beta + N_txt⊤​β+Nt​, uses the same building blocks as the classical model: a deterministic or stochastic trend and no seasonality, Fourier terms, seasonal dummies or seasonal ARIMA errors. Three design choices matter.

  • Dummies are Fourier terms. With K=s/2K = s/2K=s/2 (six for monthly data) the Fourier terms span exactly the same space as s−1s - 1s−1 seasonal dummies, so the dummy and Fourier models are nested. This is why m1 and m2 (or m3 and m4) of the classical approach give almost the same fit with K=5K = 5K=5.
  • The calendar comes first. The windows and effect types are selected before the seasonal structure. With a poor calendar component, a seasonal ARMA error absorbs the holiday misfit and looks far better than it is: in sapi, the advantage of seasonal ARMA errors over the best deterministic seasonality falls from 67 AICc points with a dummy calendar to 14 with the selected calendar.
  • Tests choose the trend, cross-validation chooses the seasonality. Information criteria cannot compare models with different orders of differencing. lunarcast therefore lets KPSS tests decide the trend, as auto.arima() does: a stochastic trend (differencing) is always a candidate, and a deterministic linear trend only when the tests indicate that the series is trend-stationary, because extrapolating a straight line is risky when the slope of the series has changed (as in sapi, which rose until 2019 and fell afterwards). The seasonal structure is then chosen by forecasting, since a good in-sample fit is not a good forecast: lunarcast refits every candidate at the end of each of the last cv_years years (five by default), forecasts the next year with the full calendar machinery and computes the RMSE. By default it applies the one-standard-error rule: among the structures whose cross-validated RMSE is within one standard error of the best, it takes the simplest,

c∗=arg⁡min⁡c{κc:CVc≤CVmin⁡+SEmin⁡},c^\ast = \arg\min_c \{\kappa_c : \mathrm{CV}_c \le \mathrm{CV}_{\min} + \mathrm{SE}_{\min}\},c∗=argcmin​{κc​:CVc​≤CVmin​+SEmin​},

where κc\kappa_cκc​ counts the structural parameters (trend slope, Fourier or dummy columns, seasonal ARMA terms). Simple structures are less likely to repeat last year’s anomalies. With trend = "cv" the deterministic and stochastic trends are compared by cross-validation as well. Every model, including those refitted during cross-validation, is required to be stationary and invertible; a fit that is not is re-estimated by maximum likelihood.

8. Outliers

With outliers = TRUE, lunarcast looks for additive outliers (ω I(t=τ)\omega\,I(t = \tau)ωI(t=τ)), level shifts (ω I(t≥τ)\omega\,I(t \ge \tau)ωI(t≥τ)) and temporary changes (ω 0.7t−τI(t≥τ)\omega\,0.7^{t-\tau} I(t \ge \tau)ω0.7t−τI(t≥τ)) among the largest robustly standardised residuals, and keeps them while ∣t∣>3.5|t| > 3.5∣t∣>3.5. Months covered by a two-level holiday are excluded, because an outlier there would be indistinguishable from that year’s holiday effect.

9. Forecasting

Future holiday regressors are built from the holiday dates. Past occurrences keep their estimated effects (this matters when a forecast starts in the middle of a holiday window), and new occurrences receive the level-2 forecast α^h,J+1\hat\alpha_{h,J+1}α^h,J+1​. The point forecast is y^T+ℓ=exp⁡(z^T+ℓ)\hat y_{T+\ell} = \exp(\hat z_{T+\ell})y^​T+ℓ​=exp(z^T+ℓ​), which is the median of the forecast distribution, and the forecast variance on the log scale is

Var(z^T+ℓ)=σℓ2+∑mm MSE^g w~h,J+m,T+ℓ2,\mathrm{Var}(\hat z_{T+\ell}) = \sigma^2_{\ell} + \sum_{m} m\,\widehat{\mathrm{MSE}}_g\,\tilde w_{h,J+m,T+\ell}^2,Var(z^T+ℓ​)=σℓ2​+m∑​mMSEg​w~h,J+m,T+ℓ2​,

the ARIMA variance plus the uncertainty of the effect of each new occurrence mmm in that month. Scenarios fix the next effect instead of forecasting it, for example forecast(fit, level2 = list(adha = log(2))) for an Eid al-Adha that doubles the level.

10. The classical model is a special case

lunarcast reduces exactly to the classical model when

  • the series is modelled on its original scale (lambda = NULL),
  • the window is the holiday month itself (window = c(0, 0)),
  • the effect is constant (effect = "constant"),
  • only one holiday is used, and
  • the trend and seasonal structure are fixed.

Every difference between the two models is therefore a choice that lunarcast makes from the data instead of assuming it. In the same way, lunarcast with the windows of the classical window design, e.g. window = list(adha = list(before = c(-14, -1), holiday = c(0, 3))), constant effects and lambda = NULL has the calendar regressors of calvar_classic(design = "window").

11. The algorithm in one list

lunarcast() runs these steps:

  1. Choose a preliminary trend type with KPSS tests on the series adjusted for a simple calendar.
  2. For every holiday, choose a constant or two-level effect by AICc.
  3. For every holiday, choose the window (ah,bh)(a_h, b_h)(ah​,bh​) on a grid by AICc.
  4. Optionally, detect outliers outside the two-level holiday periods.
  5. Choose the trend type with KPSS tests and the seasonal structure by calendar-aware cross-validation with the one-standard-error rule.
  6. Fit the final model by maximum likelihood, with the ARIMA order chosen by auto.arima() and the window shares mapped to the log scale.
  7. Choose the level-2 rule for every two-level holiday.
